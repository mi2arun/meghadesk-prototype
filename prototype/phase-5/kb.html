How refunds work and typical timeframes

Updated 12 days ago · MR Marcus Reed · en-GB · 5 min read
AI noticed Stripe updated their batch retry behavior 3 days ago. This article may need an update.

When a customer requests a refund, this is what happens behind the scenes — and what timelines you should set their expectations against.

The high-level flow

Each refund moves through a fixed pipeline. Most complete cleanly; the rest fail in ways our team can usually fix within 24h.

  1. Refund request arrives via email, chat, voice or WhatsApp.
  2. Agent verifies refund eligibility against the criteria below.
  3. Refund is issued via the Stripe API and gets a TXN- reference.
  4. Stripe submits the charge to the customer's bank batch processor.
  5. Bank approves (most common) or rejects.
  6. If rejected, Stripe auto-retries on the next batch and we monitor the result.

Typical timeframes by card type

The biggest source of confusion for customers is timing — especially for international cards where the bank's batch can run only twice a week. Be specific when you commit to a number.

  • Visa / Mastercard — 5-10 business days
  • American Express — 3-5 business days
  • Debit cards — same-day to 3 business days
  • International cards — up to 14 business days
  • Bank transfers — 2-4 business days

What can go wrong

Bank batch rejection

The most common failure. The bank's batch processor returned the refund, usually because of insufficient batch funds at the moment of submission. Stripe re-submits within 24h on its own; we don't need to do anything except update the customer. See related: Stripe batch processor failures · runbook.

Card was canceled

If the card was canceled between purchase and refund, the funds sit in escrow with the card issuer. The customer must contact their bank directly — we cannot redirect refunds to a different card.

Currency conversion issue

Rare. If the original charge was in a different currency than the customer's card, FX changes can leave a small balance discrepancy. Manual review by the payments team is required.

When to escalate

Most refunds resolve themselves on the standard timeline. There are two cases that should always be escalated:

Refunds over €500 OR for VIP-tagged customers must be escalated to the #stripe-ops Slack channel. Include the TXN- ID and the original charge date.

For example, a TXN-7732-AC refund of €148.40 on a non-VIP customer is fine to handle on the Tier 1 desk. The same refund on a VIP needs an Ops handoff.
